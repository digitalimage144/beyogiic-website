5 gentle yoga poses for lower back stiffness and pain

By Kiirti Sharma24 November 20265 min read

Hours at a desk, long commutes and evenings on the sofa all ask the same muscles to hold still for too long. The result is that familiar dull tightness across the lower back.

The good news: a few minutes of gentle, mindful movement each day can make a real difference to how your back feels. These five poses are beginner-friendly, need nothing more than a mat, and take about ten minutes together.

Before you begin

  • Move slowly. Stiffness responds to patience, not force.
  • Let your breath lead. Breathe in and out through the nose, and never hold your breath to push deeper.
  • Mild stretch, never pain. If anything feels sharp, pinching or electric, come out of the pose.

01 Child's Pose (Balasana)

A soft, restful stretch for the whole length of the spine.

Kneel with big toes touching and knees apart. Fold forward, resting your forehead down and stretching your arms ahead. Breathe into the back of your ribs for 6–8 slow breaths.

02 Cat–Cow (Marjaryasana–Bitilasana)

Gently mobilises each part of the spine and warms the back muscles.

On hands and knees, breathe in as you lower your belly and lift your gaze (cow). Breathe out as you round your back and tuck your chin (cat). Flow for 8–10 rounds.

03 Sphinx Pose (Salamba Bhujangasana)

A gentle backbend that counteracts hours of sitting hunched forward.

Lie on your front with elbows under your shoulders and forearms on the mat. Press lightly through the forearms, lengthen your chest forward and relax your glutes. Stay for 5 breaths.

04 Supine Twist (Supta Matsyendrasana)

Releases tension around the lower back and hips.

Lie on your back, hug your knees in, then let them drop to the right while your arms open wide. Keep both shoulders heavy. Stay for 6 breaths, then switch sides.

05 Bridge Pose (Setu Bandhasana)

Strengthens the glutes and back muscles that support your spine.

Lie on your back with knees bent and feet hip-width apart. Press into your feet to lift your hips, hold for 5 breaths, and lower slowly. Repeat 3 times.

Make it a daily ritual

Consistency matters more than intensity. Try this sequence every morning, or as a reset after work. Many people find their back feels looser within a couple of weeks of regular practice.

Your spine is designed to move. Give it a few kind minutes each day.
When to seek help: if your pain is severe, follows an injury, travels down your leg, or comes with numbness or tingling, please see a doctor before starting any exercise. A personalised yoga therapy plan can then be built around their advice.
